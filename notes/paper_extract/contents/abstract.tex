Autonomous vehicles rely on onboard sensors whose perception is limited by occlusion. Buildings, trucks, and road infrastructure hide hazards until it is too late to react. A nearby edge server with roadside sensors can overcome this limit by fusing observations from multiple vantage points and publishing a shared world model to connected vehicles over V2X links. However, the published state reaches the vehicle's planner with variable delay, jitter, and potential reordering, and the planner must act on whatever arrives. We empirically characterize the \emph{operational safety envelope} of edge-assisted control, defined as the conditions under which the planner can safely consume edge state, indexed by the tail age-of-information at use time ($p99(\Delta_{\mathrm{use}})$). Using closed-loop CARLA experiments with up to 16 connected vehicles sharing a single edge tracking pipeline, we evaluate three edge perception architectures: a single-source oracle baseline, a multi-camera late-fusion stack, and an implementation of VIPS~\cite{vips2022}.

We find that multi-source stacks exhibit a sharp safety cliff at ${\sim}100\text{--}150\,\mathrm{ms}$ end-to-end latency, well before the physics-limited boundary at ${\sim}300\text{--}400\,\mathrm{ms}$ where vehicle dynamics preclude collision avoidance. The early cliff is logic-induced. Under asynchronous multi-source delivery, the edge tracker can maintain duplicate tracks for the ego vehicle, which the planner misinterprets as obstacles. We call this state-consistency failure \emph{self-ghosting}. The single-source oracle confirms the diagnosis, producing zero self-ghost events at all latencies and failing only at the physics bound. To eliminate this failure class, we introduce \emph{self-beacon anchoring}, a lightweight protocol that enforces an ego-uniqueness invariant at the edge publish boundary. In trace-driven simulations grounded in real C-V2X measurements, anchoring eliminates self-ghosting, extends the safety envelope by ${>}2{\times}$ %\KR{@Tyler: do you mean "safety envelope" when you say "safe operating boundary"? , and sustains this expanded envelope as the number of participating vehicles scales from 1 to 16.
